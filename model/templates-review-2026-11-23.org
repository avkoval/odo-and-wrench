#+TITLE: Schedule templates — review of the researched intervals
#+DATE: 2026-11-23

Review note for decision D8 ([[file:review-2026-11-21-first-cut.org]]): the three schedule templates were researched
from public sources and wait for the owner's review before use. Each item in the JSON files carries its own note and
source indexes; this file summarises the choices, the places where sources disagreed and the open questions.

Files:
- [[file:../assets/templates/subaru-forester-sh-petrol.json]] — 16 items, 9 sources
- [[file:../assets/templates/ford-transit-mk7-diesel.json]] — 14 items, 6 sources
- [[file:../assets/templates/lpg-system.json]] — 6 items, 6 sources (add-on)

"Soon window" is empty where the app default (1000 km / 30 days) is enough. Every interval is "whichever comes
first".

[[https://ivangrigoryev.com/en/maintenance-schedule-subaru-forester/][ivangrigoryev.com]] — the table has a separate

[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_FB_engine][Wikipedia: Subaru FB engine]] and the
[[https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/746538/subaru-2010-forester.html?page=338][2010 Forester owner's manual p. 338]].

** Where sources disagreed and what was chosen
1. [@1]  Timing belt: 105 000 km (manual, CIS and Israel column; ZR) vs 160 000 km (manual,
   rest of Europe; Subaru Canada for 2012+ EJ engines). Chosen 105 000 km / 7 years: Ukraine is closer to the CIS
   schedule, and the EJ is an interference engine. The kit includes the hydraulic tensioner, all idlers and the water
   pump (driven by the timing belt).
2. [@2]  Engine generation: the 2011 facelift brought the FB20/FB25 engine with a timing chain in many markets. The
   template matches by engine (EJ20, EJ25) so that an FB car does not get a timing-belt item; years stay 2008–2013 as
   requested.
3. [@3]  ATF: maker 105 000 km vs Za Rulem 30 000 km. Chosen 60 000 km / 4 years (partial change).
4. [@4]  Coolant: maker's Super Coolant intervals are long (11 years first), but for a 15-year-old car the coolant
   type is unknown. Chosen 60 000 km / 4 years; 120 000 km / 6 years is acceptable with documented genuine coolant.
5. [@5]  Oil: maker 15 000 km; chosen 10 000 km / 12 months because the manual's severe-condition list (cold, short
   trips) applies to daily use in Ukraine.
6. [@6]  Spark plugs: kept at the maker's 105 000 km for the petrol template; on LPG they should be about 30 000 km
   (GazMax: inspect every 10 000, replace 20 000–30 000). The owner's car runs on LPG, so this item should be edited
   when the LPG add-on is applied.

** Open questions for the owner
7. [@7]  Gearbox of the owner's Forester: manual or automatic? Delete either =gearbox-oil= or =front-differential-oil=
   + =atf=.
8. [@8]  Timing belt history: when and at what mileage was the belt kit last replaced, and was the water pump included?
   That sets the first due date.
9. [@9]  Coolant: is genuine Subaru coolant documented? If yes, the interval can be 120 000 km / 6 years.
10. [@10]  Spark plugs on LPG: shorten to 30 000 km here, or keep 105 000 km and rely on the LPG template note?
11. [@11]  Valve clearances: LPG is known to be harder on valves; one LPG system maker's manual (seen only in a search
   result, not verified) suggests checking valve clearance every 30 000 km on gas. Should a valve-clearance check be
   added for the LPG car?
12. [@12]  Power steering fluid: keep the item (no maker interval) or remove it?

* Ford Transit Mk7 (2006–2014), 2.2 / 2.4 TDCi diesel

Main sources: Ford's service plans per engine code as published by
[[https://uk.haynes.com/pages/maintenance-plans-car-ford-transit-2006-2014][Haynes maintenance plans]] (Euro 4 with and
without DPF, Euro 5) and the Haynes
[[https://uk.haynes.com/blogs/tips-tutorials/ford-transit-routine-maintenance-guide-2006-to-2013-models][routine
maintenance guide]] (Haynes' own minimum intervals). Timing chain and fuel filter from the
[[https://fordtransit.org/forum/viewtopic.php?f=5&t=3765][Ford Transit Forum: timing chain]],
[[https://fordtransit.org/forum/viewtopic.php?f=5&t=21793][Ford Transit Forum: fuel filter]] and
[[https://pmmonline.co.uk/technical/replacement-timing-kit-installation/][Professional Motor Mechanic]]; coolant also
from [[https://cararac.com/coolant/ford/transit.html][cararac.com]].

Timing drive: the 2.2 and 2.4 Duratorq "Puma" engines use an oil-lubricated (wet) timing chain, no belt. Ford gives no
replacement interval; failures reported on the forum and in the trade press come from worn or contaminated oil
starving the hydraulic tensioner, at around 100 000 miles. So the template has a /check/ item, not a replacement.

** Where sources disagreed and what was chosen
13. [@13]  Oil interval: Ford 30 000 km / 12 months (Euro 4), 25 000 km / 12 months (Euro 4 + DPF), 50 000 km /
   24 months (Euro 5) vs Haynes 7500 miles / 6 months. Chosen 10 000 km / 12 months — the chain tensioner depends on
   oil condition.
14. [@14]  Fuel filter: Ford has no fixed interval (blocked-filter indicator on the filter head); Haynes says every
   30 000 miles / 2 years; forum users change it with every oil change, but one warns that very frequent changes can
   distort the filter head. Chosen 20 000 km / 12 months, in autumn, plus a water drain twice a year.
15. [@15]  Coolant: Haynes 2 years, cararac 3–5 years; a search snippet attributed a 10-year Ford interval, which could
   not be opened and verified. Chosen 4 years / 100 000 km.
16. [@16]  Gearbox and rear axle: no replacement interval in any source found (level checks only). Chosen
   100 000 km / 8 years as a practical value for an old van.

** Not included, by design
- DPF: no maker interval for the 2.2 TDCi; it regenerates while driving. Euro 5 (2011+) vans may have one.
- Glow plugs, clutch, dual-mass flywheel, injectors: wear items without a maker interval.

** Open questions for the owner
17. [@17]  Is the owner's 2011 Transit front- or rear-wheel drive? (FWD: delete =rear-differential-oil=.)
18. [@18]  Euro 4 or Euro 5, and is a DPF fitted? This changes the oil specification (low-ash oil with a DPF).
19. [@19]  Is there a record of a timing chain or tensioner replacement? Does the engine rattle on a cold start?
20. [@20]  Engine names in =match=: the template uses ="2.2 TDCi"= / ="2.4 TDCi"=; the car's registered engine string
   (e.g. "2.2 diesel") has to match it — to be settled in the =template-suggestions= slice.

** Where sources disagreed and what was chosen
21. [@21]  Filters: one item for the liquid-phase and vapour-phase filters, because both are replaced at the same visit
   and the installer gives one interval (8000 km). Published values are longer (10 000–15 000 km). A third filter
   inside some reducers has a much longer interval and is covered by the reducer service.
22. [@22]  Reducer repair kit: ~30 000 km (GazMax) vs 50 000–60 000 km (GBO.UA). Chosen: a reducer /service/ every
   30 000 km / 2 years, with the membrane kit when the service finds it needed.
23. [@23]  Tank life: 10, 12 or 20 years depending on the source; the binding value is on the tank's plate. The
   2-year recertification is consistent across the sources found.
24. [@24]  Spark plugs: not an item here (kept in the car template), but the template note says to shorten the car's
   spark-plug interval to about 30 000 km.

** Open questions for the owner
25. [@25]  Which LPG system is installed (make of ECU, reducer, injectors)? Its maker's manual may give its own
   intervals.
26. [@26]  Is the 2-year tank recertification enforced for private cars in Ukraine today? The sources are LPG
   workshops, not the regulation text; the regulation could not be found and verified.
27. [@27]  Diagnostics every 6 months: acceptable, or once a year together with a filter change?

* What could not be verified
- No original Subaru Europe or Ukraine owner's manual PDF could be opened; the European schedule comes from a faithful
  transcription (ivangrigoryev.com). Several pages returned HTTP 403 (carwiki.de, autodoc, carsguide, lpgtech.ua,
  profigas.ua, gaz-time.ua, service-gas.com.ua, manualslib), so they are not cited.
- Ford's own fuel filter, coolant and gearbox-oil intervals for the Mk7 could not be read from a Ford document; the
  values come from Haynes and forum posts.
- No Ukrainian regulation text on LPG tank recertification was found; the 2-year period comes from LPG workshop pages.
